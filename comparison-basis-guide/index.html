Comparison Basis Guide

Confirm currency, quantity, options, time point, and included items all match before comparing any numbers.

Compare like with like

Two numbers can be compared only when they describe the same kind of thing on the same basis. If any one of the bases differs, the comparison does not hold.

The five things that must match

  • Currency.

    Two amounts in different currencies are not comparable until they are brought onto one basis.

  • Quantity.

    A per-item figure and a per-batch figure are not the same measurement.

  • Options.

    Different colors, sizes, versions, or conditions change what is being measured.

  • Time point.

    Figures from different moments are different figures.

  • Included items.

    What the number covers — shipping, fees, or accessories included or excluded — must be the same.

This site performs no calculation

Nothing here computes totals, converts currency, or declares a winner. The goal is only to check whether the basis is common before anyone else tries to compare. If the basis differs, the comparison stops there.

Same basis, then compare. Different basis, then stop.

A worked reading

  1. Read both numbers with their four contexts.
  2. Check the five bases — currency, quantity, options, time point, included items.
  3. Any mismatch → do not compare, mark the basis as unresolved.
  4. All match → only then is a comparison meaningful.

Numeric-reading example — no real price, fee, percentage, calculation, or verification.

?Open item: is any pair of numbers being compared while one of the five bases still differs?
