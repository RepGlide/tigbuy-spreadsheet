Percentage Claim Guide

Why a percentage also needs a denominator, a scope, the calculation time, and a source before it can be read or ranked.

A percentage is a fraction with a missing bottom

A percentage is a share of something. If you do not know what the something is, the share tells you nothing. Before a percentage means anything, its base must be stated.

What a percentage also needs

  • Denominator — the base.

    What the percentage is a share of. Without the base, the share cannot be read.

  • Scope and range.

    What the percentage applies to, and over which set of items or cases.

  • Calculation time.

    When the percentage was computed. A figure changes when its base changes.

  • Source.

    Where the percentage comes from, so it can be checked at its origin.

Percentages cannot be ranked without a base

Two percentages with different denominators, different scopes, or different times are not comparable. A larger number is only larger if it is a share of the same kind of base, measured at the same moment.

A percentage without its base is a claim without a reference. Mark it “Not stated” until the base is known.

A worked reading

  1. Read the figure and its four number contexts.
  2. Name the denominator — what the share is of.
  3. Name the scope and time — what it covers and when it held.
  4. Name the source — where to check it. Leave anything missing as “Not stated.”

Numeric-reading example — no real price, fee, percentage, calculation, or verification.
